Battery Management

Every battery is tested, numbered and tracked. Break new batteries in before competition, check internal resistance with a Battery Beak, and retire the weakest ones each year.

01Safety

WARNING

Robot batteries can deliver hundreds of amps. Never short the terminals, never use a cracked or swollen battery, and tape exposed leads when moving a battery off the robot.

02Receiving new batteries

  1. Label each new battery with a team number and ID, for example 5199-B14.
  2. Break it in: run it down to 10.5 V on the computerized battery analyzer (CBA) four times before it is used at a competition. Chief Delphi battery threads
  3. Expect a new battery to settle after its first few matches.

03Moving leads to new batteries

When new batteries arrive, move leads from the worst old batteries:

  1. Fully charge every existing battery (over 100 percent on the Beak).
  2. Note each battery's internal resistance (IR).
  3. Pick the batteries with the highest IR, unscrew their leads and move them to the new batteries.
  4. If a battery's screen or indicator doesn't work after the move, tell a mentor.

04Testing before use

  1. Check every battery with the Battery Beak before it goes on the robot.
  2. Target internal resistance below 0.02 Ω. Chief Delphi battery threads
  3. For a load test: plug into the load tester, hold the button for under a second, photograph the needle while held and after release, and send both to the Electrical Lead.

05Charging

  1. Charge on the battery cart only, never on a table.
  2. Rotate batteries so the same ones aren't always used first.
  3. At events, keep a match-to-battery log so a weak battery can be found quickly.

06Retiring batteries

  1. Buy 2–3 new batteries each year and cycle out the oldest. Chief Delphi battery threads
  2. Retire any battery with high IR that doesn't improve, damage, or swelling.
  3. Mark retired batteries "PRACTICE ONLY" or recycle them properly.
