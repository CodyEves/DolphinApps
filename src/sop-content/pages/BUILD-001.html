Kickoff and Game Analysis

Kickoff turns a brand-new game into a Will Do list. Everyone reads the rules, finds the win conditions, maps every robot action, estimates points at each level of play, and only then starts prototyping.

01Purpose

Strategy drives design. This SOP is the kickoff flow from the game reveal to the first prototyping breakouts. Alumni and mentors join the student groups; they do not split off and decide for the team. Team rule

02Kickoff gates

1
Gate 1

Understand the game

  1. Watch the game reveal together.
  2. Name the game type and list similar past games (FRC, FTC, VEX and others).
2
Gate 2

Know the rules

  1. Jigsaw: each student gets a manual section and notes robot-defining, strategy-defining and general game rules.
  2. Students with the same section meet for 10–15 minutes to agree on what it says.
  3. Mixed groups (one person per section) teach each other, highlighting the most important rules.
  4. Scenario cards: groups decide whether each build or match scenario is legal and cite the rule.
3
Gate 3

Win conditions

  1. How do you win an event (usually double-elimination playoffs)?
  2. How are teams ranked (ranking points and tie breakers)?
  3. How do you win a match: how points are scored and what penalties cost.
4
Gate 4

Every robot action

  1. Each group lists every task a robot could do, sorted by match phase, on its own tab of a shared doc.
  2. Combine the lists into one, check for gaps, and flag anything that may break a rule.
5
Gate 5

Levels of play

  1. How will the game play at week 1, week 6, district championship, championship and Einstein?
  2. What can every top robot do? What must winning robots do? What are the archetypes and the likely meta?
  3. Run simulated matches on the practice field: tape key zones, use students on chairs or dollies, time cycles.
  4. Estimate points per match at each level, using simulator tools if available.
6
Gate 6

Will Do list

  1. Agree on the Will Do list: what the robot definitely must do.
  2. Write the Might Do list: things that need testing before they go on the Will Do list.
  3. List the unknown variables that need prototyping, most important first.
  4. Everyone takes the rules quiz.
  5. Break into prototyping and design groups.
